Reading this

  1. Rejection is all-or-nothing across the fan-out. The setting belongs to the full queue, but a rejected message goes to none of the queues. The evidence is per message: each rejected sequence number is checked against every sequence each subscriber handled.
  2. never contains the damage but hides it. Healthy queues keep flowing, and the full queue loses messages with no publisher-side signal. If you use it, alert on the broker's per-queue discard counter (maxMsgSpoolUsageExceededDiscardedMsgCount).
  3. A larger quota buys time; it isn't a fix. The control backlog grew at about 0.54 MB/s, so by extrapolation its 1000 MB quota would last roughly 31 minutes at this rate. The useful questions are how long a subscriber can be down before the limit is reached, and whether an alert fires first.
  4. Missed means this publisher doesn't retry, by design for this lab: it logs and continues. A retrying publisher could avoid the loss, but those messages would still reach nobody until the full queue had room.
  5. Limits of this run. One run per scenario on a local Docker broker at a fixed rate, with handlers doing no work. The small quota compresses time; the counts scale with rate and outage length and aren't predictions for any other system. VPN-level spool limits are out of scope.
